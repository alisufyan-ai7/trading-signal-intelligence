## Objective

Recruit the first real VowSig research-beta cohort without relying on the
owner's existing personal network, collect 10–20 completed real-user feedback
responses, and establish the first evidence about customer value and
willingness to pay.

This is product/customer research. It is not evidence that VowSig helps users
earn money, and outreach must not promise profits or imply validated trading
performance.

## Target participant profile

Prioritize people who:

- actively follow or trade at least one of XAU/USD, NASDAQ 100 or DOW 30;
- already use MT5, TradingView or a similar market-monitoring workflow;
- consume or evaluate trading signals, market-analysis tools, alerts or AI
  trading content;
- can spend 5–10 minutes inspecting the VowSig beta and one recent AI decision;
- are willing to give concrete feedback on clarity, trust, missing information,
  desired markets/alerts and willingness to pay.

Avoid recruiting people merely because they are friends or because they will
give positive feedback.

## Recruitment channels

Use a staged approach rather than broad spam.

1. **Direct 1:1 outreach to active traders in public communities.**
   Target people who publicly discuss one of the supported markets. Use the
   platform's normal DM/contact mechanism where permitted.
2. **Community-admin / moderator permission.**
   Ask an admin/moderator to allow one transparent research-beta recruitment
   post rather than repeatedly posting links.
3. **Small public beta recruitment post.**
   Clearly state that VowSig is research-beta/shadow-only, no broker execution
   occurs, no trade-follow request is being made, and the goal is product
   feedback.
4. **Referral from a respondent.**
   After a useful submission, optionally ask whether they know one other active
   trader who would be willing to review the beta. Do not create a paid
   affiliate/referral program at this stage.

Specific external communities/people are not named in this document because
the current project evidence does not contain a verified live prospect list.

## Recruitment funnel and evidence states

Do not treat "message attempted" as "reached."

Track each prospect as:

`identified -> message sent -> delivery/acceptance known -> seen/opened when the platform exposes it -> replied -> beta visited -> feedback submitted -> optional follow-up completed`

Only the platform can expose delivery/read state. A sent message does not
guarantee delivery, reading or response.

For Issue #9, the meaningful cohort count is completed real-user feedback, not
outreach attempts.

## Operational volume

Start with approximately 30–40 highly relevant individual outreach attempts.

If fewer than five useful completions arrive after the first 5–7 days, review
the message/channel friction and expand by another 20–40 relevant prospects.

The objective is 10–20 completed real-user submissions, not an arbitrary
number of messages.

These outreach-volume numbers are an operating plan, not a response-rate claim.

## Expected timeline

If active direct outreach begins immediately:

- Days 1–2: prospect sourcing and first 15–20 messages;
- Days 3–5: second outreach batch and follow-ups to people who replied;
- Days 5–7: first interim evidence review;
- Days 7–14: reach 10–20 completed submissions if the channels/message work;
- after the first aggregate review: revise onboarding/product copy and feed
  evidence into Issue #8 Phase 8B.

If cold outreach performs poorly, the first cohort may take 2–4 weeks. The
process should not be prolonged merely to hit 20 responses if a smaller set has
already identified obvious usability defects; fix those first, then resume.

## Why this is worth doing now

The next major decisions — pricing, alerts, market expansion, customer account
features, usage credits, paid tiers and Phase 8B content — are expensive to
guess.

A small real-user cohort can determine whether:

- users understand the product;
- the evidence/history increases trust;
- the AI reasoning is useful;
- alerts matter enough to build next;
- users want more markets;
- the product is sufficiently valuable to pay for.

This reduces the risk of building a TradingTop-like commercial stack before
knowing which parts users actually value.

## Pricing hypothesis

The observed TradingTop research in this repository shows an entry paid plan
of $69/month and higher quarterly/annual/lifetime offerings. That is competitor
pricing evidence only; it is not proof that VowSig can command the same price
or that TradingTop users receive profitable value.

For VowSig, treat $25–$100/month as a **testable range**, not a conclusion.

Initial pricing hypotheses:

- around **$25–$35/month**: plausible early paid-beta range if users value
  trustworthy signals/evidence but the product remains narrow;
- around **$49–$69/month**: plausible if users repeatedly use the product and
  value alerts, reasoning/history and the supported markets;
- around **$99/month**: should require materially stronger recurring value,
  such as broader useful coverage, timely alerts, higher usage limits,
  richer analysis/workflow or other validated premium functionality.

Do not sell on "this helps you earn money" until there is appropriate evidence.
A subscription can be valuable because it improves decision quality, saves
time, filters bad setups or provides trusted evidence, but no profit outcome
should be promised.

## How to test willingness to pay

First cohort:

- ask whether they would pay if the evidence remains credible;
- capture their own acceptable monthly price or existing feedback price range;
- ask what exact capability makes that price worthwhile;
- separate "sounds reasonable" from "I would actually subscribe."

Second step after the first aggregate review:

- define a small paid pilot;
- choose one simple price or a very small number of offers;
- measure actual paid conversion and continued use.

Actual payment/retention is stronger evidence than stated willingness to pay.
